# Modification & Build Recipes — Evidence-Based

## Reliable stock-turbo street target
Goal: preserve response and daily drivability while improving repeatability.

Foundation: health baseline, no unresolved DTCs, verified cooling system, good plugs/coils/fuel system, high-quality tires and brakes. Thermal: intercooler sized for repeat pulls/hot climate. Calibration: reputable Focus ST-specific tune with fuel-specific map and datalog review. Mounting: rear motor mount selected around acceptable NVH. Exhaust/intake: add only where data, sound or future turbo plan justifies them.

Independent tuner case studies repeatedly place well-prepared stock-turbo cars in the high-200s and occasionally around 300 WHP, especially with ethanol blends, but dynos/fuel/ambient conditions vary. The garage therefore uses WHP as an observed result, not a guaranteed package specification.

## ~300-WHP daily-driver decision
If repeatable ~300 WHP on pump fuel is a hard requirement rather than a best-case stock-turbo result, a small responsive upgraded turbo can provide more compressor headroom at the same target. This can reduce the need to operate the stock turbo at its limit, but adds hardware, calibration and potential fueling requirements. Compare spool, torque curve, fuel requirement, intercooler demand, emissions status, cost and rollback path—not just peak number.

## Rear motor mount
Expected benefit: less engine/trans movement and wheel hop. Expected trade: increased NVH, particularly at idle and low RPM. Track the actual mount, bushing stiffness/design, break-in and user NVH rating.

## Intercooler
Community/tuner evidence strongly supports an intercooler as a foundational tuned/hot-weather modification. Verification metrics: ambient, charge temperature at start/end of pull, recovery between pulls, ignition correction and repeatable power.

## Track preparation
Do not treat horsepower as the first track upgrade. Prioritize brake fluid/pads, tire condition/pressure, wheel torque, cooling, leak inspection and post-session measurements. Community track builds repeatedly report brake-fluid boil/pad glazing and thermal management as early limitations.
